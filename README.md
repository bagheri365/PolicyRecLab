# PolicyRecLab

A research lab for off-policy evaluation, exploration, and policy-generated
bias in contextual-bandit recommendation systems.

> **Research question:** When can a target policy be reliably evaluated from
> data generated by a different logging policy?

PolicyRecLab is a controlled experimental study. Each milestone introduces one
failure mode or correction mechanism, measures it, and preserves the evidence
— including failed and rejected experiments.

## Why this project exists

Offline policy evaluation is not only an estimator-selection problem. Reliable
evaluation also depends on whether the logging process supports the target
policy, how concentrated the importance weights are, where propensities come
from, whether policy selection reuses evaluation data, and whether the
inference procedure matches the logging process.

The project separates three layers:

1. **Identification** — is the target value learnable from the logged data
   under the stated assumptions?
2. **Estimation** — how do IPS, SNIPS, DM, DR, clipping, and reward/propensity
   models behave in finite samples?
3. **Reliability** — what diagnostics and reporting are required before a
   point estimate should be trusted?

## Core estimand

\[
V(\pi_e)=
\mathbb{E}_{X}\left[
\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}(X)}
\pi_e(a\mid X)\mu(X,a)
\right].
\]

The synthetic simulator exposes exact finite-population policy value, making it
possible to measure bias, variance, RMSE, support failure, and estimator
sensitivity against ground truth.

## Experimental progression

`simulation → exposure bias → support → IPS → weak overlap → clipping/SNIPS → exploration → DM → DR → policy selection → feedback loops → reliability reporting`

Retained findings include:

- deterministic logging creates literal zero-propensity regions;
- unsupported target policies are not nonparametrically identified;
- IPS can be unbiased under correct propensities/support and still have extreme
  variance under weak overlap;
- clipping trades variance for bias;
- SNIPS is not universally better than IPS;
- DM can be stable but biased under reward-model misspecification;
- DR does not repair zero support without extra assumptions;
- reusing one OPE sample for policy selection and final evaluation induces
  winner's-curse behavior;
- stable weights or high ESS do not prove successful debiasing.

## Real-data validation

### Open Bandit Dataset

OBD is used as a one-step production contextual-bandit benchmark with supplied
logging propensities and an independent Random-policy deployment.

Across the `all`, `men`, and `women` campaigns, IPS estimates of Uniform Random
from BTS logs differed from Random-policy CTRs by about **−1.0%**, **+2.5%**,
and **−4.4%**. Yet ESS fractions remained below 0.2%, and maximum weights
exceeded 12,000.

> **Close point-estimate agreement can coexist with weak overlap.**

### Coat

Coat is an MNAR rating-exposure benchmark, not a contextual-bandit log.

The observational mean is **2.6115**, compared with **2.2289** in the
randomized subset. Item-frequency weighting removes about **18%** of the gap.
A cross-fitted latent exposure model removes only about **3%** despite stable
weights, so it is retained as a negative result. A feature-aware model removes
about **63%**, while the released propensity file removes about **73%**.

### Yahoo R3 MNAR calibration results

Yahoo R3 is also an MNAR rating-exposure benchmark.

The observational mean is **2.892**, compared with **1.820** on held-out
randomized ratings. Item-frequency weighting removes only **13.9%** of the
discrepancy.

A rating-dependent Naive-Bayes propensity model nearly reconstructs the
randomized rating distribution, but this success is structurally tied to the calibration subset rather than showing that observational data alone identify the MNAR mechanism.

Across 50 randomized splits, the fraction of runs with absolute bias at most
0.02 rises from **72% at 5% calibration** to **94% at 10%** and **96% at 20%**.
For this benchmark, 10% randomized calibration is a defensible empirical
operating point — not a universal threshold.

See [`docs/v1_3_7_yahoo_r3_results_synthesis.md`](docs/v1_3_7_yahoo_r3_results_synthesis.md).

## Reliability rule

> **Never report an offline policy value without also reporting whether the
> logged data could reasonably identify it.**

A complete report should state the estimand, logging process, propensity
source, support condition, target-policy selection procedure, and inference
method. ESS is a weight-concentration diagnostic, not a literal inferential
sample size.

## Philosophy

`baseline → measurable failure → targeted intervention → evaluate → retain / reject`

Change one adaptation mechanism, measure it, and preserve the evidence —
including failed and invalidated experiments.
