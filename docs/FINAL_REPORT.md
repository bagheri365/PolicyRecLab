# PolicyRecLab final research report

## Abstract

PolicyRecLab studies when a target policy can be reliably evaluated from data
generated by another logging policy. The project uses exact-value synthetic
contextual-bandit experiments, Open Bandit Dataset as a production
contextual-bandit replication, and Coat/Yahoo R3 as separate MNAR
rating-exposure benchmarks.

The main conclusion is that offline evaluation is not an estimator-selection
problem alone. Identification, overlap, propensity provenance, policy
selection, and inference assumptions determine whether a point estimate is
meaningful.

## 1. Research question and estimand

> **When can a target policy be reliably evaluated from data generated by a
> different logging policy?**

For one-step contextual-bandit OPE,

\[
V(\pi_e)=
\mathbb{E}_{X}\left[
\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}(X)}
\pi_e(a\mid X)\mu(X,a)
\right].
\]

## 2. Identification assumptions

The baseline contextual-bandit analysis requires consistency, valid logging
randomization or conditional exchangeability, positivity/support, valid
propensity information, a final target policy fixed independently of its final
evaluation sample, and inference matched to the logging process.

If the target policy assigns probability to actions that the behavior policy
never takes, the target value is **not nonparametrically identified** from the
logged data under the baseline assumptions.

Identification, estimation, and inference are treated as distinct layers.

## 3. Synthetic laboratory

### 3.1 Exposure and support

The logging policy determines which actions and rewards become observable.
Deterministic greedy logging creates zero-propensity regions, so unsupported
target policies are a structural identification failure rather than merely a
high-variance estimation problem.

### 3.2 IPS, overlap, clipping, and SNIPS

IPS can be unbiased under correct propensities and support while still having
extreme finite-sample variance under weak overlap. Clipping trades variance for
bias. SNIPS is finite-sample biased in general and is not uniformly better than
IPS.

### 3.3 DM and DR

DM can reduce variance but becomes biased under reward-model misspecification.
DR can tolerate one nuisance-model failure under standard conditions, but it
does not repair zero support without extra extrapolation assumptions.

### 3.4 Exploration, policy selection, and feedback loops

Exploration changes both online reward and future evaluability. Reusing the
same OPE sample for policy selection and final evaluation creates
winner's-curse behavior. Policy-generated data can also alter future support
and amplify observation bias.

## 4. Open Bandit Dataset

OBD is a production contextual-bandit benchmark with supplied logging
propensities and an independent Random-policy deployment.

| Campaign | Random reference CTR | IPS estimate | Relative difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| all | 0.003469 | 0.003434 | −1.0% |
| men | 0.005124 | 0.005253 | +2.5% |
| women | 0.004815 | 0.004603 | −4.4% |

Despite plausible point estimates, ESS fractions are below 0.2% and maximum
weights exceed 12,000.

> **Close point-estimate agreement can coexist with weak overlap.**

## 5. Coat MNAR benchmark

Coat is an MNAR rating-exposure benchmark, not a contextual-bandit log.

The observational mean is 2.6115 versus 2.2289 in the randomized subset.
Item-frequency weighting removes about 18.3% of the discrepancy.

A cross-fitted latent exposure model has ESS around 99.3% but removes only
about 3% of the discrepancy. It is **rejected as an effective debiaser** and
retained as a negative result.

A feature-aware model removes about 63.2% of the discrepancy, while the
released propensity file removes about 73.4%.

> **Weight stability is not evidence of successful debiasing.**

## 6. Yahoo R3 MNAR benchmark

Yahoo R3 contains 311,704 self-selected ratings and 54,000 randomized ratings.
The observational mean is 2.892, compared with 1.820 on held-out randomized
ratings. Item-frequency weighting removes only 13.9% of the discrepancy.

The rating-dependent Naive-Bayes model uses randomized calibration data to
estimate the target marginal rating distribution. Its weighted mean is close
to the randomized reference, but that success has a structural interpretation:
inverse weighting reconstructs the calibration rating distribution. It does **not** demonstrate that the observational data alone identify the MNAR mechanism.

Across 50 randomized splits:

| Calibration | P(|bias| ≤ .02) | 95th pct absolute bias |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1% | 28% | 0.0902 |
| 2.5% | 58% | 0.0554 |
| 5% | 72% | 0.0413 |
| 10% | 94% | 0.0220 |
| 20% | 96% | 0.0170 |

10% randomized calibration is a defensible empirical operating point for this
benchmark. It is not a general law about calibration budgets.

## 7. Cross-benchmark synthesis

### 7.1 Identification comes first

A more sophisticated estimator cannot repair missing support.

### 7.2 Diagnostics must accompany point estimates

Weak overlap, concentrated weights, propensity-model failure, and
selection-induced optimism can all coexist with plausible-looking estimates.

### 7.3 Benchmark categories remain distinct

OBD is a production contextual-bandit benchmark. Coat and Yahoo R3 are MNAR rating-exposure benchmarks. Their common theme is policy/exposure-generated observation bias, not identical causal structure.

### 7.4 Negative results matter

The rejected Coat latent exposure model demonstrates that stable weights and
high ESS do not imply successful debiasing.

## 8. Reporting standard

Every reported offline policy value should state:

- the estimand;
- the logging process;
- the propensity source;
- the support/positivity condition;
- the target-policy selection procedure;
- the inference method.

ESS is a concentration diagnostic, not a literal inferential sample size.

## 9. Limitations

- The core OPE scope is one-step contextual bandits, not general sequential RL.
- OBD Random-policy CTR is an empirical reference, not exact truth.
- Coat/Yahoo randomized ratings are empirical references under a different
  data-generating process.
- Yahoo calibration uses randomized information directly.
- Adaptive logging requires inference matched to the sequential process.

## 10. Final conclusion

> **A target policy can be reliably evaluated from a different logging policy
> only when the target estimand is identified under the logging process and the
> estimate is accompanied by evidence about support, propensity quality,
> selection procedure, and finite-sample reliability.**

> **Never report an offline policy value without also reporting whether the
> logged data could reasonably identify it.**
